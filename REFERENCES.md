# References

This file lists what each part of the scene is based on. Anything marked **(plausible)** is not confirmed by a source. It is a choice that fits 1980s Newar Patan.

No measured site plan of the square was found online. Positions and sizes in `source/src/square/layout.js` are **estimates**. They come from the known north-to-south order of the monuments, the typical size of each temple type, tourist maps and modern satellite views. Every position is accurate to a few metres at best.

## Monuments

All temples on the west side face east toward the palace. North to south:

| Monument | Based on | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bhimsen Temple | Wikipedia; period photos | Rectangular three-roofed pagoda, rebuilt 1682. Low brick plinth, front toward the square. Footprint 9 x 12 m, height 17 m (estimated). |
| Vishwanath Temple | Wikipedia; period photos | 1627, two roofs, stone elephants flanking the east stairs. Tall stepped plinth. Height 15 m (estimated). |
| Krishna Mandir | Wikipedia; EAP166 | 1637, stone shikhara with three levels of open pavilions. Garuda kneels on a pillar facing it from the east. Height 20 m (estimated). |
| Char Narayan (Jagannarayan) Temple | Wikipedia | 1565, two roofs on a tall stepped brick plinth guarded by stone lions. Height 15.5 m (estimated). |
| Hari Shankar Temple | Wikipedia | Three-roofed pagoda, 1704–05. Height 16 m (estimated). |
| Octagonal Krishna Temple (Chyasim Deval) | Wikipedia | 1723, octagonal stone shikhara at the south end, facing Sundari Chowk. Height 14 m (estimated). |
| King Yoganarendra Malla's Pillar | Wikipedia | 1693. The kneeling king in gilded bronze on a tall stone column, under a cobra hood, facing the palace. Column height 11 m (estimated). |
| Garuda Pillar | Wikipedia | Kneeling Garuda on a low column facing Krishna Mandir. |
| Taleju Bell | Wikipedia | 1736. A big bell hung between two stone posts, in front of the palace. |
| Manga Hiti | Wikipedia | Sunken stone water spout at the north end, with makara-head spouts. Its exact outline is **(plausible)** until step 5. |
| Mani Mandap (two pavilions) | Wikipedia | 1700. Raised stone platforms with open wooden pavilions beside Manga Hiti. Their exact position west of Manga Hiti is estimated. |
| Royal Palace: Keshav Narayan Chowk | WMF; asianart.com | Northernmost courtyard, rebuilt 1734, golden gate on the square. Shown as the palace wing it was in the 1980s, not as the later Patan Museum. Courtyard modeled as a façade only, to save effort. |
| Royal Palace: Mul Chowk | WMF; KVPT | 1666, central courtyard. Façade only. |
| Royal Palace: Sundari Chowk | KVPT | 1647, southern courtyard around the sunken Tusha Hiti bath. **Walkable.** Its entrance from the square is **(plausible)**: the real way in is through the palace. |
| Degutale Temple | Wikipedia | Tall roof tower rising from the palace between Mul Chowk and Keshav Narayan Chowk. Exact position estimated. |
| Taleju octagonal tower | Wikipedia | Octagonal three-roofed tower at the north-west corner of Mul Chowk, overlooking the square. Part of the palace, not a separate monument file. |

Left out to keep the scope small (they exist, but are not modeled): Bhai Dega, the small Krishna temple near the bell, and the smaller shrines and chaityas around the square.

## Architecture parts

| Part | Based on | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Roof struts (tunala) | Modern close-ups of Patan struts | A many-armed deity on a small attendant, under a canopy. The figures are generic **(plausible)**. Each strut does not show its real, individual deity. |
| Eave bells and leaf clappers | Period photos of Patan pagodas | Spacing is about 1.1 m **(plausible)**. |
| Pataka banner | Photos of Patan temples | Follows the roof slopes down the front. Its width is an estimate. |
| Doorways and toranas | Modern close-ups | Three nested carved frames, lintels reaching past the jambs. The torana is gilded on the front door and wooden on the sides **(plausible)**. |
| Stair guardians | Photos of Char Narayan and Vishwanath | Seated lions and elephants are simplified. |
| Krishna Mandir tiers | EAP166, Wikipedia | Two colonnaded storeys with kiosks crowned by small shikharas, and the central tower. Pillar counts are approximate. |
| Paving | Period photos | Brick herringbone with dust-filled joints and worn paths. |
